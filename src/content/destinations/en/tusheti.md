From the Alazani valley a dirt road climbs to the Abano Pass, at about 2,850 m, and drops into Tusheti: stone villages, slate towers and summer pastures with no through traffic. It is a 4×4-only road.

## Getting there

The journey starts from Telavi in [Kakheti](/destinations/kakheti): about 100 km and four to five hours by 4×4. The road is dirt and exposed, so the vehicle and driver matter more than the schedule. Rezo uses a short 4×4 Sprinter (see [our vehicles](/vehicles)), and he plans the day around the conditions rather than the clock. Read more on the [4×4 adventure page](/adventure-4x4).

## When the road is open

Snow decides when the road opens each year. It is **usually open June to October**; Tusheti days are therefore planned around the season, and if you ask for Tusheti outside it, Rezo will say so and suggest other high valleys instead. The [mountain roads guide](/guides/georgia-mountain-roads-guide) explains what to expect.

## Omalo and the villages

Omalo (about 1,880 m) is the main village and the usual base. Beyond it the valleys are walking country: stone villages, slate towers and high pastures. Allow at least two nights, because the road in and out takes most of a day.

## Fitting Tusheti into a route

Tusheti is a long way from everywhere. After it, a night in [Tbilisi](/destinations/tbilisi) makes the drive to the west ([Kutaisi](/destinations/kutaisi-imereti)) easy. For a shorter plan, combine it with Kakheti’s cellars in the eight-day [Wine valleys to high villages](/journeys/wine-valleys-to-high-villages) idea.
